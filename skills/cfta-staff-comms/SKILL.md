# CFTA staff communications

All brett-prose rules apply. The additions below are specific to internal
CFTA staff writing.

## Tone

Direct, respectful, and plain. Not corporate-formal, not casual. CFTA staff
are professionals who can handle straightforward communication. Do not
over-explain or over-soften.

## Urgency

Do not imply urgency that does not exist. Avoid "critical", "urgent",
"immediately", and "as soon as possible" unless the situation genuinely
requires it. Overuse trains staff to ignore urgency signals.

When something is actually time-sensitive, say the actual deadline: "please
respond by Friday, May 2" is more useful than "please respond urgently."

## Specificity

Name specific dates, roles, and next steps. Vague action items get ignored.

Bad: "Please follow up with the relevant team members at your earliest
convenience."

Good: "Please send your responses to Brett by Friday, May 2. If you have
questions, contact the operations team."

## Delivering difficult news

Lead with the fact. Then give context. Then say what comes next. Do not bury
the lead in softening language.

Bad: "As you may be aware, the organization has been navigating some
significant changes, and after careful consideration and consultation with
leadership, we have made the difficult decision to..."

Good: "We are eliminating the coordinator role effective June 30. Here is
what that means for the team and what happens next."

## Voice

Use "we" for organizational decisions. Use "I" for personal decisions.
Do not attribute organizational decisions to "leadership" or "the team" as
a way to diffuse accountability. If the organization made a decision, say so.

## Format

Short paragraphs. One topic per paragraph. If a message covers multiple
distinct topics, use headings to separate them.

For messages that require staff to take action, end with a clear summary of
what is being asked, from whom, and by when.
