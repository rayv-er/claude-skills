# Board memo style

All brett-prose rules apply. The rules below are additions specific to
board-level writing.

## Structure

Executive summary first, every time. The board reads the first paragraph and
skims everything else. The opening paragraph must stand alone: someone who
reads only it should understand what is happening and what (if anything) is
being asked of them.

Standard structure for most board documents:

1. Summary (what is happening and what you need)
2. Context (background, if the reader needs it to evaluate the ask)
3. Recommendation or decision requested
4. Supporting detail

## The ask

Make the ask explicit. If you need a vote, an approval, or a decision, say so
in the first paragraph. Name the specific action: "The board is asked to
approve the revised investment policy" or "This is for information only; no
action is required."

Do not bury the ask in paragraph four.

## Voice

Use "the board" in third person when referring to the board as an institution.
Use "we" for organizational decisions and "I" for personal ones.

Avoid operational detail in board documents. The board governs; staff
operates. Describe outcomes and decisions, not the work that produced them.

## Numbers

Financial figures always include the period of measurement and a comparison:
vs. budget, vs. prior year, vs. the same period last year. A bare number with
no context is not useful to a board member.

Bad: "Revenue was $1.2M."
Good: "Revenue was $1.2M for Q1, $80K under budget and $140K ahead of Q1
last year."

## Attachments

Label attachments referenced in the body ("see Attachment A"). List all
attachments at the end of the document with a one-line description of each.

## Length

Board members are busy. Say what needs to be said and stop. The summary
paragraph is not a place for hedging or context-building. That belongs in
the body, where board members who want it will find it.
